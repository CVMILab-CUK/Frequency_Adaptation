A Frequency Dial for Structure-Conditioned Diffusion

One adapter, trained on conditions whose bandwidth is randomised, so a single cutoff controls how tightly generation follows the reference — at inference, without retraining.

What the cutoff does

The condition is the image after a radial high-pass filter with normalised cutoff r. At r=0 the model receives the grayscale image and the task is colourisation. As r grows the filter removes more of the spectrum. Natural images concentrate their energy at low frequency, so this happens fast:

Training draws r per sample from a uniform distribution, so one set of weights covers the whole range and the cutoff becomes an inference-time parameter.

Turning the adapter down is not the same knob

Holding r=0.1 and scaling both injection paths instead reaches comparable structure consistency at much worse image quality. At scale 0.50 the model scores 0.2146 with FID 76.46; at r=0.3 it scores 0.2347 with FID 52.77. Weakening the adapter degrades the image; narrowing the condition's bandwidth does not.

Two findings from building it

The frozen VAE destroys the condition. Encoding a high-pass condition with Stable Diffusion's autoencoder, as an IP-Adapter-style design would, leaves 0.58 of it at r=0.3 and 0.07 at r=0.7. No adapter can recover what the autoencoder already discarded, so the condition encoder reads the image directly.

Placement beats capacity. Residuals on the decoder skip connections carry 0.5002 of the structure signal against 0.1744 for cross-attention injection. Quadrupling adapter width raises structure consistency from 0.149 to 0.283 and doubling again reaches only 0.299; a 7×7 attention window is worse than 3×3.

Where it does not hold

Read before citing the numbers

The useful range is roughly r ∈ [0, 0.3]. Above it the condition retains too little energy for structure consistency to mean much, since two nearly empty signals correlate near zero whatever the model does. Everything here is one domain (FFHQ faces) and one backbone (SD1.5); current structure-control work has moved to DiT backbones where LoRA-style adapters report control at a fraction of our 6.6M parameters. Per-cutoff specialists still beat the single model at r=0.1. No user study was run.

Frequency decomposition in diffusion is usually applied to the model's own signals rather than to its condition. Randomising the condition's bandwidth during training, so the band becomes a control at inference, is the part we did not find elsewhere.
