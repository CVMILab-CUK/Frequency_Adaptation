A structure-conditioned diffusion adapter is trained for one operating point. Canny edges, a depth map, a sketch: each fixes how tightly the output must follow the condition, and moving that trade-off means training again or scaling the adapter’s strength at inference. We train a single adapter on high-pass-filtered image conditions and draw the cutoff r per sample, so the information in the condition varies during training and r becomes a dial at test time. Trained this way, how tightly an output follows its structure is set by the condition while the weights stay fixed. On FFHQ-512, a single adapter moves structure consistency from 0.7469 at r=0.05 to 0.2347 at r=0.3, each output scored at its own cutoff, and from 0.6524 to 0.2599 with the instrument fixed at rm=0.1, while FID stays between 51.58 and 54.68 over r ∈[0.05,0.3]. Scaling this adapter’s strength instead pays for lower structure consistency with FID: read with the same fixed instrument, scale 0.50 reaches 0.2146, a little below the cutoff r=0.3, and gains diversity, but raises FID to 76.46 against 52.77. On hand drawings filtered at r, the output’s correlation with the drawing also falls as r grows.
