A structure-conditioned diffusion adapter is trained for one operating point. How closely its output follows the condition is fixed at training, and loosening it later means another checkpoint or a lower adapter strength. We move that choice into the condition. A single adapter on Stable Diffusion 1.5 is trained on grayscale images passed through a radial high-pass filter, with the cutoff r drawn per sample. At test time r becomes a dial, much as image-editing tools let users pick a frequency band. On FFHQ-512, raising r from 0.05 to 0.3 lowers structure consistency (mean of three training seeds) from 0.7479 to 0.2318 while FID stays between 51.58 and 54.68. In our samples the face layout holds while background, clothing and colour drift from the reference. Lowering the adapter's strength instead pays for looser structure with FID: at strength 0.50 structure is looser than at r = 0.3 under the same fixed instrument, and FID is 76.46 against 52.77. At r = 0.1, an adapter trained only for that cutoff still follows the structure more closely. Injecting through the UNet skip tensors carries most of the structure, and an artist can filter a hand drawing the same way; the output's correlation with the drawing falls as r grows.
